# UI

The browser UI of the git and Debugging tracks (Nuxt 4 SPA, Nuxt UI 4, Tailwind 4). Decisions: `docs/adr/0001-plan.md` (layout, terminal, graph), `docs/adr/0003-look.md` (look) and `docs/adr/0004-editor-layouts.md` (the two layouts, CodeMirror). Pure logic is in `app/utils/` (unit tests in `test/unit/ui/`); the engine and the git model stay in `shared/` and know nothing about the UI.

## Pages

| Route | File | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `/` | `app/pages/index.vue` | Home, all tracks (see "Home"). |
| `/<track>` | `app/pages/[track]/index.vue` | A live track's page, e.g. `/git` (`TrackOverview`, see "Track page"); a coming or unknown track is a 404. |
| `/<track>/<slug>` | `app/pages/[track]/[level].vue` | The play page: it picks the level's track's play component (Git: `GitPlay`, Debugging: `DebuggingPlay`; see "Play page"). The slug is the level id without the track: `/git/01-first-commit` for `git-01-first-commit` (`app/utils/level-route.ts`). An unknown slug shows the 404 page. |
| `/git/cheat-sheet` | `app/pages/git/cheat-sheet.vue` | The cheat sheet; "Practise it in" links each command to its levels by number (`3.3 Merge conflict`). |
| `/debugging/cheat-sheet` | `app/pages/debugging/cheat-sheet.vue` | The Debugging cheat sheet (see "Debugging cheat sheet"). |
| `/about` | `app/pages/about.vue` | What makes it different, how it works, your progress (backup and reset, see below), coming later, who builds it. |

Navigation (`app/layouts/default.vue`, `navigationItems` in `app/utils/navigation.ts`): one list for the header, the mobile menu and the footer: **Tracks** (`/`), one entry per live track from the registry (**Git**, active on its page, its levels and its cheat sheet; **Debugging**, the same menu: Levels and Cheat sheet, since 0.2.15), **About**. The cheat sheet is reached from its track (the rail and the home card), not from the global nav. A new live track needs data only: nav, home card, track page and level routes follow from `shared/levels/tracks.ts`. From md the list sits in the header; below md a menu button opens it (Nuxt UI's header menu).

## Track page (`TrackOverview`)

The whole track and your place in it on one page: a chapter rail plus chapter sections (`.impeccable/surfaces/app-pages-git-index-vue.md`). Pure view model: `trackView(track, progress)` in `app/utils/track-view.ts` (tested on the real track and the 58-level fixture).

- **Rail** (from lg, sticky under the header, 17rem / 18.75rem from xl, scrolls itself on short screens with a thin scrollbar; the radar shrinks to 192px when the window is under 56rem tall, so the rail fits at about 900px height): track title with the track ⋯ menu, "85 levels in 8 chapters · scope" (the counts come from the registry), the track's progress bar and "n of N solved", the skill radar (230px, centred; see "Skill radar" below), the chapters (`ChapterNav`: commit dot, name, mini bar in the chapter's colour, n/m, or "Coming"), the cheat sheet link. A click scrolls to the section (smooth, instant under reduced motion), puts `#chapter` in the URL and moves the focus to the section heading. The chapter being read has `aria-current` (`useActiveSection`: the last section whose top passed a line under the header; at the end of the page the last one; a click pins its chapter until you scroll by hand).
- **Below lg**: the rail's heading and the radar (200px) scroll away with the page; the chapters become a strip of chips (`[data-chapter-strip]`) that sticks under the header and scrolls sideways; the current chip is scrolled into view. No horizontal page scroll down to 360px.
- **Continue card** (`ContinueCard`): the entry is the heading ("3.3 Merge conflict", or a practice task's title); below it a quiet line ("Where you left off in Merging · Last played 2 hours ago"; a practice task names its scenario: "Next up in Basics › 1.2 Stage only what belongs together"), the first paragraph of its story (or the task) and an ember button. Without any progress it is "Start here"; when the last played entry is solved it is "Next up"; with nothing unsolved left but an entry flagged "practise again", it is "Practise again in …". With every level solved and nothing flagged it says so and links the cheat sheet. Its layout follows its own width (container query), so it also fits the home page's narrower column.
- **Chapter sections** (`ChapterSection`): heading with the commit dot and "n of m solved" (`#<chapter-id>`, both kinds counted), the chapter ⋯ menu on the right, one-line description, the rows. A chapter without levels shows its description and a quiet "Coming" (no menu).
- **Rows** (`LevelRow`, the whole row is the link; the ⋯ menu sits beside it, outside the link, in the row's right gutter). A scenario: commit dot, number, title with the commands it practises (from the solution) as small tags, difficulty (three dots, filled up to it) and time ("~5 min"), status. A practice task (preview G2): a smaller dot on the side branch, no number, its title in regular weight, time ("~30 s"), status. Status is text: "Solved · best 3 cmds" (the best run by the track's measure: `bestRunCounters`; Git its commands as before, Debugging "best: no hints, 1 reload"), "Next up", "Not solved", plus "⚑ Practise again" in the warning colour when flagged. Tags beside the title: git the commands a level practises, Debugging the error or status a scenario teaches (`TypeError`; in Network `404`, `500`, `payload`, `CORS`, `double POST`; in State & async the mistake: `await`, `race`, `closure`, `shallow copy`, `promise chain`; spoken "Teaches: 404") or a practice task's kind (`find`, `quick fix`), as in the chapter preview. From md difficulty/time and status have their own columns (status 12rem, 16rem from xl, so the time column lines up); below md they share one line under the title. The accessible name says it all: "1.2 Stage only what belongs together, Solved, best run 4 commands. Difficulty 1 of 3, takes about 5 minutes. Practises add, commit." (a practice task: "Practice for 1.1 First commit: Stage only app.js, …").
- **Commit dots and lanes** (`CommitDot`, `LevelRow`, the signature; preview `practice-graph.html`, variant G2): an entry drawn as a commit in its chapter's colour: filled when solved, hollow when open, an ember ring when it is next up; a coming chapter is a plain grey ring. The chapter's 2px **main line** runs through the scenario dots (12px) and past the practice rows; a scenario with practice tasks forks a short **side branch** 14px to the right (a small curve out of its dot, then straight down), and its practice dots (8px, `size="xs"`) sit on it; the branch ends at the last practice task (like `git log --graph`, it does not merge back). Lanes are solid between two solved entries and dimmed around open ones (`lane: { in, out, through, fork, sideIn, sideOut }` in the view model). The one authored moment: back on the track page after solving an entry for the first time (`takeJustSolved` in `useProgress`), its lane draws in once, top-down through the new dot (CSS `transform` only, four chained steps; a scenario's on the main line, a practice task's on its branch; off under reduced motion). The rail, the strip and the section headings repeat them for chapters (filled: every entry solved; ember ring: holds next up). Always decoration (`aria-hidden`): the state is also in text.
- **⋯ menus** (`MoreMenu`, `UDropdownMenu`; actions in `useProgressActions`): an entry (row and level page): **Play again** (a fresh session), **Mark "practise again"** (a checkbox; solved entries only), **Mark as not solved** (solved entries only). A chapter: **Practise again: <chapter>** (opens the first flagged entry, disabled when none is), **Reset chapter <chapter>…**. The track (beside its title in the rail): **Reset all <track> progress…** (disabled without progress). Every reset opens the one confirmation dialog (`ConfirmDialog` in `app.vue`, `useConfirm`): the title asks ("Reset chapter Basics?"), the text says exactly what is cleared ("4 solved levels, their best runs, "practise again" flags and where you left off in Basics are cleared. This cannot be undone."), Cancel has the focus, the action is in the error colour. A reset also drops the in-memory play sessions of what it clears; an open level page restarts in place (`registerPlay` / `restartPlays` in `useProgress`).
- **Skill radar** (`SkillRadar`, values from `trackRadar`, see docs/features/levels.md "Skill radar"; approved preview `.impeccable/mocks/previews/radar-preview.html`): drawn after the logo's mark (ADR 0005). View box 220; three rings at a third, two thirds and all of a chapter and a spoke per chapter, faint (`--ui-border-accented`); axes clockwise from the top in chapter order. Your shape in ember (`text-primary`, fill 0.28, 2px stroke, round joins) with a small ember dot at each corner (an empty axis keeps a corner at 4% of the radius); each axis ends in an outlined dot in its chapter's colour (`.branch-c-N`, filled with `--dot-ground`). No labels: the chapter list is the legend. A coming chapter: dashed spoke, dashed end dot, no corner dot. Nothing solved: rings, spokes and end dots only, with "Solve levels to fill your radar" below. Hovering or focusing a chapter row lights its axis (spoke in the chapter's colour, bigger corner dot); hovering near an axis in the picture lights its row (`nearestRadarAxis`; not near the centre or far outside). The picture is `aria-hidden`; screen readers get a visually hidden list ("Basics: 4 of 11 solved", "…: coming"). No animation on the track page (DESIGN.md: the lane draw is its one animation). Geometry is pure and tested: `app/utils/skill-radar.ts`, `test/unit/ui/skill-radar.test.ts`.
- `ProgressMeter`: a thin bar in `currentColor` (a chapter colour or ember), `aria-hidden`; the count is always written next to it.

## Home

`app/pages/index.vue`, everything from the registry: a short pitch (title, intro, "no account" note); a Continue card when there is progress (`hasProgress`; the track from `trackToContinue`), beside the pitch from lg, below it on smaller screens; every live track as a `TrackCard` (title at 30px, below the page title; tagline, description, "85 levels in 8 chapters · n of 85 solved" with a bar, the primary button, the cheat sheet link when the track has one, and the chapter list linking to `/<track>#chapter`; two columns from lg). The primary button never repeats the Continue card (`trackCardAction`): "Start: 1.1 First commit" (plus See all chapters) for an untouched track, "Open Git track" once the Continue card points at it or every level is solved, "Continue: …" for a track with progress that the Continue card does not show; the coming tracks as a quiet list (two columns from md: dashed dot, title, "Coming", one line), and a link to About for what makes Grindstone different. Below lg everything stacks in that order.

## Your progress (About page)

`ProgressBackup` (ADR 0006, "Backup"): progress lives only in this browser. The section shows what is stored ("7 solved, 1 to practise again", or "Nothing saved yet.") and three actions:

- **Download progress**: the stored JSON as `<site>-progress-YYYY-MM-DD.json` (the site name from `useAppConfig().site`, lower-cased; the viewer's date; `backupFileName` in `app/utils/progress-backup.ts`). The link is appended to the page while it is clicked and the object URL is revoked a second later.
- **Load progress**: a file picker (JSON). A file over 1 MB is refused before it is read (`readBackupFile`). The file goes through `readProgress`, the same parser as the stored progress: broken JSON, a foreign file or an unknown version shows a clear error in the status line ("notes.json is not a Grindstone progress file. Nothing was changed.") and changes nothing. A valid file replaces the progress; when there is progress already, the confirmation dialog asks first (what the file holds, what it replaces). Afterwards the status line confirms what was loaded ("Loaded …: 7 solved, 1 to practise again.") and every play session starts over; when the browser would not store it (`replace()` returns false) the status line is an error instead, because the progress is gone when the page closes.
- **Reset everything…**: the confirmation dialog, then all progress is gone.

The status line is a polite `role="status"`.

Hosting: the app is static; direct links such as `/git/01-first-commit` need the host to fall back to `200.html` (`nuxt generate` writes it) for unknown paths.

## Play page

The page (`[level].vue`) finds the level and its place and hands them to its track's play component; a level of a track without one is a 404. Git's is `GitPlay` (layout switch, `GitWorkbench`, editor panel, the file git waits for, debrief); Debugging's is `DebuggingPlay` (see "Debugging play page"). What every track shares:

- `usePlaySession(level, { fresh, onRestart })`: the session in memory (`useState` under `play:<id>`, which `restartPlays` drops), the goal checklist, next level, best run, recording the solve (`useProgress().record`, best run by the track's measure), the stats events (`level-start`, `hint`, `level-solved` with the run's counts; docs/analytics.md), the spark, the debrief (opens 900 ms after a scenario's solve; `progressBefore` for the radar) and Again (also when a reset or a loaded backup restarts the page). A track's composable (`useGitPlay`) keeps its own panels' state next to the session and calls `update(session)` after every action and `solved(session)` on the one that solved it.
- `PlayFrame`: the three-column frame (level panel left, the work in the centre, a view of the result right; stacked on phones), filled through its `panel`, default and `side` slots. With `tabs` (Debugging) the right column follows the centre below lg and takes only its panel's height.
- `LevelPanel` (its conflicted-files list is optional, git only; its default slot, under the goals, is the track's own: Debugging's revealed hints and a find task's verdict) and `DebriefDialog` (the track's run goes into its `stats` slot: git's is `GitRunStats`, Debugging's `DebuggingRunStats`).

- **Left**: `LevelPanel`; a scenario's revealed hints are listed under the goals (`1/3 …`), since there is no terminal to print them in; a practice task's one hint shows in place as in git. In chapter 7 the **Messages** (`DebuggingTeam`) come first under the goals: the colleague's report the scenario opens with, then each colleague event that fired (info tint), in a polite `role="log"`, so a new message is read out where you are and the focus never moves.
- **Centre**: **Code** (`DebuggingCode`, flex 1.6; 1.1 in levels with the Elements panel or the Debugger, which need the room) above the **Console** (`DebuggingConsole`, flex 1). A level with a fake backend (chapter 2) has the **Network** panel (`DebuggingNetwork`) too, a level of chapter 3 the **Elements** panel (`DebuggingElements`), a level of chapter 5 the **Debugger** (`DebuggingDebugger`), a level of chapter 6 the **Performance** panel (`DebuggingPerformance`), a level of chapter 7 the **Logs** (`DebuggingLogs`) and **Deploys** (`DebuggingDeploys`) panels, as tabs next to the Console: a small segmented `tablist` above them (Elements, Debugger or Performance, Logs, Deploys, Console, Network: the ones the level has, the chapter's own panels first, and the first one open; named after its panels, "Console and Network", "Debugger and Console", "Performance, Console and Network", "Logs, Deploys, Console and Network"; arrow keys, Home and End), the Network tab with the number of requests, in the error colour once one failed (spoken ", some failed"). The top row's note in chapter 5: "Record a run of the app and step through it, then fix the code, Save and Reload…"; in chapter 6: "Record a run of the app and see what it costs, then fix the code, Save and Reload…"; in chapter 7: "Read the server's logs and the shop's deploys, then fix the code (or roll back), Save and Reload…".
- **Right**: the **App** (`DebuggingApp`) and the spark when a level is solved.
- **Below lg** the panels are tabs (App, Elements, Debugger or Performance, Logs, Deploys, Console, Network, Code: the ones the level has; a `tablist`, arrow keys, Home and End move; the Performance tab shows "Perf" and is named "Performance", so five tabs fit a phone; chapter 7's six tabs are two rows of three, since one row of six would cut their names), App first; all stay mounted (hidden with CSS), so a switch never reloads the app. The phone's Network tab gets a small error dot once a request failed. The two tab sets agree on the lower panel, so a resize keeps what you looked at; a panel is labelled by the tab set that shows (`useMediaQuery`). Opening a line from the Console, the Network or the Elements panel switches to Code; Pick switches to the App (scrolled into view) and, after the click, back to Elements; Record switches to the App and Stop back to the Debugger (or the Performance panel); a log line's request id switches to the Network panel (from lg too).

- **Debrief** (scenarios): `DebuggingRunStats`, this run's hints and reloads and your best run ("no hints · 1 reload"), one line on the run.

### Code

A tab per file of the app in reading order (`fileOrder`: index.html, app.js, the other modules by name, the stylesheet last; a `tablist`, arrow keys, Home and End), `●` for unsaved edits, **Save** (also Ctrl/Cmd+S; disabled with nothing to save; a save of unchanged text does nothing). `LazyCodeEditor` with HTML and CSS highlighting (`@codemirror/lang-html`, `lang-css`, each its own chunk). A line opened from the Console is marked like the browser does (error tint and a bar on its left edge, `mark`) and scrolled to the middle; the first edit removes the mark inside the editor's own transaction (a separate clearing transaction mid-edit ended Firefox's composition and lost the inserted text). **Find tasks**: the editor is read-only and every line is an answer: a click on it or its number, or Enter on the cursor's line (`pickable`); the answer is marked success or warning. Saved changes run only after Reload: until then the App says "You saved changes. Reload to run them." **With the Debugger** (chapter 5) a gutter of dots sits left of the line numbers: a click on it or on a line number, or Ctrl/Cmd+B on the cursor's line, sets or removes a breakpoint (a dot in the info colour, the warning colour with a condition; they stay on their line numbers through edits, like the panel lists them; in a find task the line itself still answers, its number sets a breakpoint), and the line the Debugger is at is lit (info tint and a bar, its number bold) and scrolled into view; moving to a step in another file opens that file. The editor's name then says how to set a breakpoint.

### Console

`DebuggingConsole`: the app's `console.log/info/warn/error/debug`, uncaught errors and unhandled rejections, as the bridge reports them (`consoleEntry`). Errors are red (`bg-error/8`), warnings amber, each with an icon; the source link (`cart.js:17`) sits on the right, an error's frames below it ("at addToCart (cart.js:17)"): only frames in the level's files (the bridge, the browser and eval'd input are left out). Every link opens the file at that line and marks it; in a find task a link is also the answer. A syntax error has no frames, only where the browser found it. **Clear** empties it; a Reload empties it too (as the browser's Console does by default). The **input line** runs an expression inside the app (`›` your input, `←` the value as a one-line preview like the browser's: `{items: Array(0), count: 1}`, `ƒ formatPrice(amount)`, a promise `Promise {…}` and inside an object `price: Promise`; an object literal works as in DevTools, and so does a top-level `await` (`await priceOf('croissant')` shows what the promise settles to, also with a trailing `;`; a rejection is red; `const total = await …` defines `total` for later inputs, as a property of the window); an error is red). Up and Down walk this visit's inputs. It is a polite `role="log"`, each line has a hidden level word ("Error: …"); at most 500 lines. No filters or groups yet (ADR 0007).

### Network

`DebuggingNetwork`, in levels with a fake backend (ADR 0007, chapter 2): the requests the app sent, in the order sent, from the pure entries in `shared/levels/debugging/network.ts` (`requestEntry`, `withResponse`, `statusLabel`, `statusTone`, `durationText`, `bodyView`, `requestName`).

- **Header**: Network, "3 requests", **Clear**. A Reload empties it too, like the Console (`useDebuggingPlay` keeps the list per load; at most 200 requests a load).
- **List** (a `listbox`, Up and Down, Home and End move the selection, which shows the details): method, name (as the browser's panel names it: the last part of the path with the query, `search?q=cro`, `menu`; the host when the path is empty; the full URL is its tooltip and in the details), status and time. The name never hides: below 24rem of list width (phones, and beside the details) a row has two lines, the name on the first and method, status and time under it; wider, one line, and a long name wraps instead of being cut. Status as the browser's panel words it: the code (`404`), `pending` with a spinner, `CORS error`, `failed`, `canceled`; coloured `text-success` (2xx), `text-error` (4xx, 5xx, CORS, failed), muted otherwise. Each option's name says it all, with the full URL ("GET https://cornerbakery.shop/api/products, 404 Not Found, 31 ms").
- **Details** of the selected request (a focusable region): the method and full URL; Status ("404 Not Found"), Time, **Sent from** (the initiator: the first frame of the call stack at `fetch` in the level's files; it opens the file at that line, and in a find task it is an answer, like a Console link); then **Request** and **Response**, each with its headers and body. A body is shown as text in a `<pre>`, never as HTML; JSON is indented (`bodyView`). Without a body: "No body.", "Waiting for the answer…", or why there is no answer the app could read (CORS, no connection, cancelled).
- **Layout**: a container query: from 36rem wide the list and the details sit side by side (each scrolls); narrower (phones), the details follow the list in one scroll area, so the list and its keyboard stay where they are. No horizontal page scroll at 360px (long URLs and bodies wrap).

### Elements

`DebuggingElements` (with `DebuggingRule` for one rule), in levels with `sandbox.elements` (ADR 0007, chapter 3): read-only, fixes happen in the code. The data and the cascade: docs/features/levels.md, "The Elements panel".

- **Header**: Elements, **Pick** (a toggle, `aria-pressed`, ember while on), **Refresh** (a new snapshot of the page as it is now; the tree is not live, like a snapshot after each load).
- **Tree** (a `tree` of `treeitem`s with `aria-level` / `aria-expanded`, `aria-activedescendant`; Up and Down, Home and End move, Right opens or goes in, Left closes or goes out; the selection follows): each element as markup, `<button id="order" class="order">` with its own short text, tag, attribute names and values in their colours; an element without a box is dimmed and spoken "not shown"; one whose content is wider than its box gets a `scroll` badge (spoken "its content sticks out"), like the browser's. `<html>`, `<body>` and what is in it start open. Hovering a row outlines its element in the app, leaving the tree goes back to the selected one; the outline shows only while the panel is the one on screen. A snapshot over 600 elements says the page has more.
- **Pick**: the App shows "Click an element in the app to select it. Esc stops." with Stop; the next click in the app selects what it lands on (the app never gets that click), the tree opens to it, scrolls it into view and takes the focus. While picking, what is under the pointer is outlined in the app and lit in the tree. Escape (in the app or on the page) stops.
- **Details** of the selected element (a focusable region; side by side with the tree from 36rem of panel width, below it narrower, like Network): its name; **Styles**: `element.style`, then the matching rules from the winner down, each with its `@media` / `@supports` lines (linked), its selector list (the selectors that matched bright, the others dimmed), its specificity (`1 · 1 · 0`, spoken "specificity: 1 ids, 1 classes, 0 types") and a link to where it is written (`style.css:99`, opens the line in Code; in a find task it is an answer); each declaration is a link to its own line too (in a find task an answer); declarations struck through when they lost (spoken "overridden"), struck through with a warning when the browser refuses them, and "--brown is not defined" under a declaration that uses a variable nothing defines; then **Inherited from** each ancestor that passes something down (only that). A line says the browser's defaults are not listed. **Box**: DevTools' nested margin, border, padding and content with their sides (a dash for 0; one sentence for screen readers), "No box" without one, and "Its content is 512 px wide in a 340 px box: it sticks out." when it does. **Computed**: the chapter's values (display, visibility, opacity, position, z-index, pointer-events, color, background-color, width, height, min-width, overflow, text-overflow, white-space). An element that is gone since the snapshot says so (Refresh).
- **Across reloads** the selected element stays selected when the new page has it at the same place in the tree (`nodePath`), so you see the new styles of what you were looking at.
- Everything from the frame is text (tag names, attributes, texts, selectors, values); no colour swatches (a value from the frame never becomes a style here).

### Debugger

`DebuggingDebugger` (with `DebuggingValue` for one variable or property), in levels with `sandbox.debugger` (ADR 0007, chapter 5): a recorded run to step through, never a paused app. The data and the stepping model: docs/features/levels.md, "The recording".

- **Header**: Debugger, while recording the number of steps so far, and **Record** (**Record again** once there is a recording; disabled while a Reload's check run is on) or **Stop** (error colour, spinning while the last steps come in, at most 600 ms). Before any recording the panel says what Record does and how to set breakpoints.
- **Record** loads the chapter 5 tools (acorn and the instrumentation, their own chunk; if they can't load, a warning line in the panel says so (offline?) and nothing else changes), reloads the app you see from the saved files with its code instrumented (not counted: no reload, no check run) and records until Stop or a cap. While recording the App shows "Recording: use the app, then Stop." with the step count and its own Stop; on a phone Record switches to the App tab and Stop back to the Debugger. Any other load of the app (Reload, Reset, Again) ends the recording; the breakpoints stay for as long as you are on the page.
- **Once recorded**: an info line "Recorded run: the app isn't paused. You move through what it already did, and see each value as it was then." Under it, when it applies: the recording stopped at its cap ("…after 2000 steps, the most it keeps") or because values were too slow to read; a file that ran without steps and why (a syntax error, too long, too deep, a function declared twice or with a var's name in one body); the code in the editor differs from what was recorded ("Save, then Record again"). It opens at the first step a breakpoint stops at, else at the first step.
- **Controls** (a `toolbar`): Back to the previous breakpoint, Step back, **Resume** (to the next breakpoint; with none ahead to the end, and a line says so), Step over, Step into, Step out; icons with names and tooltips. Keys like the browser's DevTools, on the whole page while there is a recording and no dialog is open: F8 or Ctrl+\\ resume, F10 or Ctrl+' step over, Ctrl+; step into, Shift+F11 or Ctrl+Shift+; step out. Plain F11 stays the browser's full screen, and Shift+F8 and Shift+F10 (the context menu) stay the browser's too. When the debrief closes, the focus goes to the first control there is: the toolbar's, else Record, else Stop (recording); so it does after Stop (the panel's or the App's), unless it is somewhere real already (the app). A **scrubber** (`input type=range`, "Step 37 of 412"; its spoken value names the file and line). After each move a polite live region says where it is ("Step 37 of 412: cart.js, line 14.") and what the move ended on.
- **Where it is**: the current step's `file:line` and that line's code, **Breakpoint here** / **Remove breakpoint** (the keyboard's and the phone's way to a breakpoint without the gutter), and in a find task **Answer: file:line** (the step's line is the answer, like a click on it).
- **Scope** (a `<details>` per scope: Block, Local (fn) with `this`, Closure (fn), Module, the last closed; from 36rem of panel width beside the Call stack): each variable with its value as the browser previews it, strings green, numbers and booleans amber, `undefined`/`null`/`<uninitialized>`/`(...)` dimmed, functions blue; an object opens (a `<details>`, its entries rendered only once opened, an array with its `length`, "… 12 more" when it had more).
- **Call stack**: the frames top first, each a button with the function and `file:line` (the selected one `aria-current`), an "async" line above the frames of the call that started an async function now back from an `await`. ### Sandbox and bridge

- **The frame**: `<iframe sandbox="allow-scripts" srcdoc>`, nothing else: the app's document has an opaque origin, so it can't read Grindstone's localStorage (the progress), IndexedDB or cookies, can't touch the parent page's DOM, can't navigate the top page, open popups, submit forms or show dialogs. Its document (`sandboxDocument`) has, in front of everything of index.html but its doctype (and HTML whitespace before it), on that line (so no line of index.html moves): a `<base href="https://cornerbakery.shop/">`, a Content-Security-Policy (`SANDBOX_CSP`: `default-src 'none'`; scripts only inline, blob and eval (for the Console); styles inline and data; `connect-src 'none'`, so no request leaves the frame; `base-uri 'none'`, `form-action 'none'`) and the bridge. The browser makes the `<head>` for them, and the page's own `<html>` and `<head>` tags land on it (attributes kept). So nothing of the level's files runs before the bridge: not a script before `<head>`, not a `<head>` inside a comment or a script that the bridge used to follow (since 0.3.1).
- **Modules**: the bridge makes a blob URL inside the frame for each `.js` file (so it shares the frame's sealed origin) and an import map from each file's name (and `./name`) to it. A blob URL has no folder to resolve `./cart.js` against, so relative imports of the level's files are rewritten to the files' names before (same line; an import of a file that does not exist stays as written, so the browser reports it). `<script type="module" src="./app.js">` becomes `<script type="module">import "app.js"</script>` and the stylesheet a data URL, on the same lines. Chrome then names a module `'money.js'` where it would say `'./money.js'`.
- **The bridge** (`shared/levels/debugging/bridge.ts`, one line of plain JavaScript): runs before anything of the app's. First it takes its own text off the page (the config with the load's token and the probes), keeps the browser's own functions it relies on, and makes two `MessageChannel`s: its first and only message to the window (`window.parent.postMessage`, with the load's token) hands the page one port to read the load from and one to write to it. The port to the page lives only in the bridge's closure and is only used through a `postMessage` bound before the app ran, and every message is a fresh object of plain data, never changed once made; so nothing in the frame (the app's code, Console input) can post to the page as the bridge (`e2e/debugging-cheat.spec.ts`). Over it the bridge posts `console` calls (with their call stack, or an Error argument's), uncaught errors (the error's stack, or where a syntax error was found), unhandled rejections, failed files and refused network requests, `ready`, and in a check run each probe's result and `done`. It reads only what comes over the page's port (`eval`, the Elements panel's requests, `recordStop`). Its listeners (errors, rejections, load, links, the page watch, a profile's actions) are capturing listeners on the window set before any of the app's, read the events through the browser's own getters and start the probes only on the browser's own `load`, once: the app's code can't keep an uncaught error from being reported. **The probes** use only those kept functions (the DOM's getters and methods, `getComputedStyle`, events, the timer), the config as data without prototypes and callbacks on the timer (no promise, no array method), so a built-in the app replaced or a property it put on a prototype can't change which probes run, what they do or read, or when the run is `done`. Links do not navigate the frame. **The fake backend** (docs/features/levels.md, "The fake backend"): `window.fetch` is wrapped; in a level with a backend, `BACKEND_SOURCE` answers every `fetch` inside the frame and posts each request (`request`), its answer (`response`) and the browser's own Console line for a failed one (`netlog`: `GET … 404 (Not Found)`, the CORS refusal, `net::ERR_…`); in a level with logs (chapter 7) every answer of the shop's server carries `x-request-id` and the backend posts a `serverlog` of numbers when it answers (docs/features/levels.md, "The logs"), which the page reads only for this load's own requests. Without a backend a request goes on and the CSP refuses it. A check run waits after loading and after each click until every request is answered (at most 3 s), then reads. **The inspector** (levels with the Elements panel, the app you see only): snapshots, matching rules, the outline (a closed shadow root on `<html>`, never clickable) and pick mode, answering `dom`, `inspect`, `highlight` and `pick` from the page's port only (docs/features/levels.md, "The Elements panel").
- **The page's side** (`app/utils/sandbox-host.ts`, `useDebuggingPlay`): each load gets a fresh random token and its own link (`bridgeLink`): of the window's messages it takes only the first hello from that load's iframe with that token and two ports (`isBridgeHello`), then reads the load from the first port only and sends to the bridge over the second (what is sent before the hello waits, at most `MAX_WAITING_MESSAGES`, 100); a new load closes the old link. Anything else posted to the window (by the app's code, the Console, an older load) is never read. `readBridgeMessage` keeps only well-formed messages with plain strings and numbers (texts cut at 10,000 characters, at most 50 console arguments or headers, file URLs only `blob:`, a method is capital letters, a status a whole number up to 999, a failure one of `cors`, `network`, `aborted`); everything is shown as text, never HTML. A `netlog` is a Console error whose source link is the line that sent the request (no trace of its own). Stack traces (`parseStack`: Chrome's `at fn (url:line:col)`, Firefox's and Safari's `fn@url:line:col`, Firefox's `async*`/`promise callback*` causes, Safari's `[native code]`) are mapped back to the level's files (`mapFrames`: the blob URLs, and `about:srcdoc` is index.html except the bridge's line); a message that names a module by its blob URL (Firefox) gets the file name.
- **The check run** (`runCheck`; with the level's backend, a fresh one, so a sequence of answers starts over; it keeps no request messages, the probes count requests inside the frame): a reload also runs the saved files in a second, hidden sealed frame (390 × 700 like a phone, out of sight but for one transparent, unclickable pixel column inside the viewport (`checkFrameStyle`: WebKit all but stops the timers of a cross-origin frame wholly outside it, and a check run lives on timers), `inert`, `aria-hidden`; it never keeps the keyboard: the probes type without taking the focus, and should anything in it take the focus anyway (Firefox lets a script in a sealed, inert frame do that; the page then has nothing focused), the element that had it gets it back; probes that ask for another width get a frame of that width after it, and their observations are put together) with the level's probes, so their clicks never touch the app you see; its messages become the `Observation` the goals read. It ends on `done`, or after 5 s as not finished (an endless loop or a hang fails "no errors"). A Reset or Again while it runs drops its result.
- **The recorder** (chapter 5, a recorded load only): the bridge sets it up as the global `__gs$` before any module runs; the instrumented modules call it before each statement, it posts their steps in batches (`steps`) and its end (`recordEnd`), and stops on the page's `recordStop` (over the page's port only). The page keeps steps only while it is recording, checked by `readSteps`. During a recording the Console shows messages through `withoutInstrumentation`, so what Firefox and Safari quote of the code reads as written (docs/features/levels.md, "The recording").
- **The page watch and the server's clock** (chapter 6, a Performance level's loads): a MutationObserver from the moment the page is parsed counts what the app changes (for the `changes` probes and the profile's renders), and the server's clock moves only when an answer arrives, by its scripted delay (the `waited` probes and the profile's request times). A recorded profile also reports the customer's actions (capturing listeners set before the app's) to the counting recorder, which posts a live count and, at the page's `recordStop` (over the page's port only), the profile. A check run whose probes read the work runs the code instrumented to count (`runCheck(…, { counted })`), over its own ports like any load. The changes the watch keeps for the `changes` probes, and the fake backend's record of what was sent (the `requests` and `sent` probes), are read with the functions kept before the app ran too.
- **What it does not protect against**: the app runs in Grindstone's tab, so an endless loop in it freezes the page like it would freeze a real tab. The goals trust what the bridge measures, and the app shares its JavaScript world: the app's code can always change the real page (show the expected text, written into the code; the levels' guards catch the plain cases), behave differently while it is checked (a check run's clicks are not trusted events, its frame is out of sight), or skip its own errors (catch them, replace `console.error`). What it can no longer do (0.3.1): post results or `done` itself, make the probes read anything but the page, or hide an uncaught error. The rest of the fake browser (the backend's routing and CORS, the recorder's tables, the Console's previews, the Elements panel) still uses the frame's built-ins where it runs after the app; replacing them only changes what the app itself gets, or what the visible panels show, never a check run's results. The port the page sends on can be seen by code the Console runs while the bridge handles its input (`window.event`); it only leads back to the page's sending end, which reads nothing.


## Debugging cheat sheet

`/debugging/cheat-sheet` (`app/pages/debugging/cheat-sheet.vue`, data in `app/utils/debugging-cheatsheet.ts`, texts under `debugCheat.*` in the locale file, unit test `test/unit/ui/debugging-cheatsheet.test.ts`), registered as the track's `cheatSheet` in `shared/levels/debugging/index.ts`, so the track page rail, the track card, the Continue card and the nav menu link to it from data alone (like Git's). Same frame as the Git sheet: the header's width, a filter and jump links, CSS columns of at least 20rem, one card per entry. No print styles (Git has none).

- **Groups**, one per panel or skill: Console (reading a trace, syntax errors, the input line, top-level `await`, logging), Network (status codes, status words without a code, request and response, a 404, `response.ok`, the payload, CORS, double POSTs), Elements & CSS (tree and Refresh, matched rules, specificity, undefined variables, Computed, Pick, covered buttons, the box, media queries), State & async (missing `await`, races and `AbortController`, closures, shallow copies, promise chains), Debugger (Record, breakpoints, conditions, stepping, shortcuts, Scope, Call stack), Performance (counted not timed, Functions, Renders, Requests), Incidents (Logs, deploys and diffs, which deploy, Roll back, feature flags, hand-built JSON). A last group, **Error messages**, has one card per message the levels quote in Chrome's wording (`TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'push')` … `AbortError: signal is aborted without reason`): the message is the heading, then "Usually means" and "Look first".
- **Entry** = a title, a summary, optional examples and "Practise it in:" links. An example is a line you click to copy (a Console input, a snippet, a selector, a header) with a `#` note above it; a key or a status word is a plain row that does not copy (`copy: false`). The links are written by hand (a list of scenario ids per entry, since the Debugging levels do not list a technique's commands the way a git solution does); the unit test pins that every id exists, is a Debugging scenario, appears once, is in level order, and that every scenario of the track is linked from somewhere. Links read like Git's: `2.1 The menu won't load`.
- **Filter** (as Git's): over the title, summary, example lines and notes of an entry, and over an error's message and both of its texts; a group with no match disappears, and "Nothing matches" shows when nothing is left.
- **Texts avoid vue-i18n syntax** (`{`, `}`, `|`, `@`), which the test checks; code with braces lives in the example lines, which are data.
- What it says follows the panels (the sections above) and the level debriefs: the shortcuts are the ones the Debugger implements (F8 or Ctrl+\, F10 or Ctrl+', Ctrl+;, Shift+F11 or Ctrl+Shift+;), Step back and Back to the previous breakpoint have none.

## Play page layouts

A segmented control above the play area (`LayoutSwitch`, a radio group: Tab reaches the chosen one, arrow keys move the choice) switches between two layouts, **Terminal** (default) and **IDE**. The choice is stored per viewer in localStorage (`grindstone:layout`, read through `parseLayout`, every access guarded; with storage blocked it lasts for the visit; a stored `mix` from the dropped Mix layout reads as `ide`) and shared by all levels (`usePlayLayout`).

Both layouts are one skeleton (`GitWorkbench`, in the shared `PlayFrame`), so a switch feels like panels opening and closing in place, not a re-shuffle: the level panel, the terminal, the commit graph and the switch keep their place. IDE only adds two panels: **Files** (`FileExplorer`) below the level panel and the **editor** (`EditorArea`) above the terminal. A closed panel is `inert` (no focus, hidden from screen readers) and takes no space. The switch sits right-aligned in its own row and never moves; the "best with a keyboard" note on its left shows on phones in both layouts and keeps its space (invisible) from md up.

## Editor

- CodeMirror 6 (`@codemirror/state`, `view`, `commands`, `language`, `@lezer/highlight`): line numbers, undo history, bracket matching, active line, soft line wrapping (no sideways scrolling on phones). Tab is not bound, so keyboard users can leave the editor. Ctrl/Cmd+S saves.
- Languages by file extension, each loaded as its own chunk the first time such a file opens: JavaScript (`js`, `mjs`, `cjs`, `jsx`), TypeScript (`ts`, `mts`, `cts`, `tsx`), JSON, Markdown, HTML (`html`, `htm`), CSS; everything else is plain text. The two files git opens during `git rebase -i` get small stream languages without a chunk: `git-rebase-todo` (command word as keyword, an unknown first word as invalid, the commit id in info blue, `#` lines as comments) and `COMMIT_EDITMSG` (`#` lines as comments).
- Theme from the app's tokens (`EditorView.theme` with `var(--ui-*)` and `--app-terminal*`), so one theme serves dark and light; syntax colours are semantic tokens (keywords secondary, strings success, numbers warning, comments dimmed, functions info, headings primary).
- **Conflicts** (`app/utils/conflicts.ts`, pure, tested): `parseConflicts(text)` finds complete `<<<<<<<` / optional `|||||||` / `=======` / `>>>>>>>` blocks (exactly seven marker characters at the start of a line; an unclosed block is ignored; a second `<<<<<<<` starts over). Current (ours) lines get the `--app-conflict-current` tint (success), incoming (theirs) `--app-conflict-incoming` (info), the marker lines a stronger tint and a "(current change)" / "(incoming change)" tag. Above each block a bar with **Accept current · Accept incoming · Accept both** (real buttons, accessible name "Conflict n: accept the current change", ...) rewrites the block in the editor buffer (`resolveConflict`; Accept both keeps ours then theirs; a diff3 base section is dropped; a block that ends the file without a line break leaves none). Nothing is saved until Save. Editing the markers by hand keeps working: the decorations follow the text.

## Sync rules (editor tabs and the engine)

The editor shows the engine's working tree (`session.state.workdir`); a tab never writes to it except through Save. Each tab keeps the disk content its draft is based on (`base`), the draft, and an optional "changed on disk" notice (`app/utils/editor-tabs.ts`, pure, tested; `useEditorTabs` applies it whenever the working tree changes):

- A command (or `undo`, `reset`, a teammate event) that leaves the file as the tab's base: nothing happens; a notice that no longer applies (e.g. after `undo`) disappears.
- The file changed and the tab has **no unsaved edits**: the tab reloads.
- The file was deleted and the tab has no unsaved edits: the tab closes.
- The file changed under **unsaved edits**: the draft stays, the tab gets a warning icon and the editor a notice with **Reload from disk** (take the disk version) and **Keep mine** (the draft stays and counts as an edit of the new disk version; saving overwrites it).
- The file was deleted under unsaved edits: the tab is struck through and marked deleted; the notice offers **Close tab** and **Keep mine** (saving brings the file back).
- `edit <file>` in the IDE opens the file in a tab or focuses its tab (on phones it scrolls the editor into view, without popping up the keyboard); a file that does not exist yet opens as an empty tab and is created on Save. In the Terminal layout `edit <file>` opens the editor panel as before.
- Save (Ctrl/Cmd+S or the Save button) writes the draft through `saveFile`, prints `Saved <path>.` in the terminal and counts as a command (undoable with `undo`). Saving a tab with a notice is the same as Keep mine plus Save.

## The file git waits for (`git rebase -i`)

Approved in the chapter 4 preview (`.impeccable/mocks/previews/rewriting-preview.html`). Like real git opening your editor:

- `git rebase -i` leaves the git state waiting for a file (`waitingEditor` in the git model); the play page derives the open editor from the session (`useGitPlay`: `gitEditor`, its `gitDraft`), so `undo`, `reset` and Again open or close it on their own. The terminal shows git's `hint: Waiting for your editor to close the file...`.
- **IDE layout**: `GitEditor` takes the editor pane's place (the tabs stay hidden but untouched until git stops waiting); on stacked layouts it scrolls into view. **Terminal layout**: `GitEditorDialog`, a modal like the `edit <file>` panel, without a close button; Escape and a click outside do nothing. Switching the layout moves it between the two and keeps the draft.
- The bar: the file name (`git-rebase-todo`, `COMMIT_EDITMSG`; the full path as tooltip), one line on what to do, **Cancel** (outline, accessible name "Cancel the rebase (git rebase --abort)") and the primary action, **Start rebase** for the todo list, **Save message** for a message (ember, the one primary action). Ctrl/Cmd+S is the primary action; Escape never cancels.
- Start rebase / Save message: `saveEditor` with the draft (counted, undoable), `Saved <path>.` in the terminal, then git's output. If git opens the next file (a reword's message after the list), the same editor shows it with git's text; a refused list (an unknown command) stays open with your text. Cancel runs `git rebase --abort` as a typed line, so the terminal shows what it did.
- Focus: the editor gets it when it opens (not on touch devices, so the keyboard does not pop up), and the terminal input gets it back once git stops waiting.

## Terminal behaviour

- Lines go to `submit(session, level, line)`; `{ kind: 'clear' }` empties the log, `{ kind: 'edit', path }` opens the file (editor panel in the Terminal layout, a tab in the IDE), notices become an inline teammate line (icon plus text, e.g. "Jonas pushed 2 commits to main.") inside the live log, so screen readers announce them. After a solve a "Level solved" line is added.
- Up/Down walk the history (kept for the whole visit, shared by all levels; the line being typed is restored at the bottom). Ctrl+L clears (like `clear`, not counted). Ctrl+C drops the current input.
- Tab completes (`app/utils/completion.ts`): trainer commands, `git` and shell commands at the start; git subcommands after `git`; branch names (local, `origin/<name>`, names on origin) for `switch`, `checkout`, `merge`, `rebase`, `branch`, `log`, `push`, `pull`, `fetch`, `diff`, `reset`, `revert`, `reflog`, `cherry-pick`, `tag`; tag names for the same commands except `pull`, `fetch` and `reflog`; `origin` for `push`/`pull`/`fetch`; file names for `add`, `checkout`, `diff`, `commit` and for `cat`, `edit`, `rm`, `touch`, `ls`. One match completes with a trailing space, several extend to the common prefix, and when nothing can be added the choices are printed. Flags are not completed. Tab on an empty line is left to the browser, so keyboard users can leave the field with Tab (and Shift+Tab always works).
- A click anywhere in the terminal focuses the input unless text is selected. After the editor or the debrief closes, the focus goes back to the terminal.
- The session lives in memory (`useState`): leaving the page and coming back keeps it, a reload restarts the level. Solving a level stores progress in localStorage (`grindstone:progress`, through `parseProgress` / `serializeProgress`, every access guarded; the app works with storage blocked, it just forgets).

## Graph layout (`app/utils/graph-layout.ts`)

`layoutGraph(input)` is a pure function: commits, local branches, `origin/*` refs, HEAD and optionally origin's own state in, rows, lanes and edges out. `CommitGraph` turns them into coordinates.

- Which commits: everything reachable from a ref (local branch, `origin/*` ref, tag, a detached HEAD, and with the toggle on origin's own branch heads). Commits no ref reaches, such as the old commits a rebase replaced, are not drawn.
- Rows: topological, children before parents, newest first (logical timestamp, then id as tie-break). Row 0 is on top.
- Lanes: one per branch line. Refs claim commits in order (`main`, the checked-out branch, other branches by name, a detached HEAD, `origin/*`, origin's own heads); a ref claims its first-parent chain until it meets a commit another ref already claimed. A branch fast-forwarded onto main therefore owns nothing and shares main's lane. Commits nobody claims (the merged-in side of a merge whose branch is gone) get their own lane named `line:<id>` with a colour from that name. Lane order is the order of claiming, so `main` is lane 0.
- Colour: the node's colour comes from its lane's branch (`origin/x` colours as `x`). A fork edge takes the colour of the branch that starts there, a merge edge (second or later parent) the colour of the merged-in line (`merge: true` on the edge).
- Labels per commit: local branches (`kind: 'local'`, filled pill, `head: true` draws `HEAD -> name`), `origin/*` refs (`remote`, outlined), a detached HEAD (`head`), with the toggle on origin's own branch heads that your `origin/*` ref does not show yet (`origin`, dashed, `origin: main`), and tags last (`tag`, `tag: v1.4.0`, neutral: muted text colour, a thin outline and the 0.25rem tag radius instead of a round pill). Tags claim no lane and no colour.
- Drawing: row height 42px, lane width 22px. A commit with labels shows the pills above and the first message line below; short id, message and labels are also in the SVG `<title>` (hover) and in a screen-reader list. HEAD is the commit with a ring. Rows move with a CSS transform transition, new rows fade in and their dot pops with a spark ring (`@starting-style`), edges animate their path (CSS `d`, where the browser supports it; otherwise they jump).
- The "Show origin" switch (only when the level has an origin) adds origin's own commits and heads, so you see what a teammate pushed before you fetch.

## Rough edges

- Phones are usable but the terminal is the whole point: type on a hardware or software keyboard; there are no command buttons.
- The graph scrolls inside its box when pills and messages are wider than the column; text width is estimated from the monospace advance (6.6px at 11px), so a fallback font can be off by a few pixels.
- Undo and Reset are terminal commands (`undo`, `reset`); only Reset has a button.
- The IDE's terminal panel has a fixed height (no drag handle yet). Tabs live as long as the play page: leaving the level and coming back keeps the session but starts with no open tabs.
- No source control panel (staged/unstaged lists, commit button): the trainer teaches the commands (ADR 0004).
